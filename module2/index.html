Content That Fits Three Ways

QUICK TAKEAWAY

In CE 205, presentation content has to work three ways at once: it meets the assignment’s instructions, fits the format (slides or a poster), and supports what you speak. Content that misses one circle is not finished.

WHAT YOU NEED TO DO

Narration

In CE 205, presentation content has to do three jobs at once. Here is the model for it.

The triangle is your content: everything on your slides or poster, and everything you say about it. Inside it are three circles. Let's zoom into each one.

INSTRUCTIONS means the assignment sheet and its rubric. Its purpose is credit. Content counts only when it covers every required element and follows every rule. So the check is simple: is each requirement visibly there?

FORMAT is the medium: slides or a poster. The same content takes a different shape in each. Slides show one idea at a time, in the order you choose. A poster shows everything at once, and it has to stand on its own. The check: does this content work in this format?

SPEAK is what you say: your speaker notes and your delivery. Its purpose is a talk that matches what the audience sees. Build the notes from the same content, keep them to short prompts, and fit them to the time limit. The check: can you say it, in time, from the same content?

One circle is not enough. Content that meets the rubric but crowds the poster, or reads well but cannot be said in time, still needs work. Aim for the centre, where all three overlap.

Your agent should work the same way. Give it your written assignment, and the presentation's instructions and rubric. It asks one question first: slides or a poster? Then it drafts the content and the speaker notes together, and checks all three circles.

For Presentation 02, the instructions call for a digital poster. So the checks become: every required element visible on the poster, a poster that stands on its own, and an elevator speech of 2 to 3 minutes, built from the same content.

Build an agent that aims for the centre.

STEP 02 OF 2BUILD AND APPLY

Build Your Agent and Use It for Presentation 02

QUICK TAKEAWAY

A one-time prompt cannot keep all three circles in view. Build an agent that reads your written assignment and the presentation’s instructions, asks whether the content is for slides or a poster, drafts the content and the speaker notes together, and checks all three circles. Then use it for Presentation 02.

WHAT YOU NEED TO DO

  • Write the agent’s instructions in your own words.
  • Run it with WA01, WA02, and the Presentation 02 sheet and rubric.
  • Check its content with the three-circle check.
  • Design the poster yourself, then rehearse with a timer.
1Your paper + the presentation’s instructions and rubric→2Slides or a poster?→3Content→4Speaker notes from the same content→5Three-circle check

Your instructions must answer

  1. Inputs. What does it ask for before drafting: your written assignment, the presentation’s instructions and rubric, the audience, and the time limit?
  2. Format. How does it choose slides or a poster? It should take the format from the assignment, and ask you when the instructions do not say.
  3. Instructions. How does it show where each required element and rubric item appears, and flag what is missing?
  4. Format check. For slides: what goes on each slide, with an informative title and a visual? For a poster: which sections, how they are arranged, and how the poster stands on its own?
  5. Speak. How does it build speaker notes from the same content: short prompts, fitted to the time limit, not a script?
  6. Never invent. Facts, numbers, sources, or images that are not in your materials.
  7. Output. What does every response return, so you can check all three circles quickly?

No template to copy: as in Module 1, writing the instructions yourself is the point.

Presentation 02: the three circles

INSTRUCTIONS

WHAT THE POSTER MUST DO

  • Describe the civil engineering catastrophe from WA02
  • Indicate the disciplines relevant to the failure
  • Explain the failure in technical detail (summarized), with graphics
  • Describe the prominent project from WA01
  • Identify transferable lessons from the success, applied to the failure

FORMAT

A DIGITAL POSTER

  • One poster, typically a single PowerPoint slide, saved as a PDF
  • Uploaded to Google Drive by the shared deadline
  • Stands alone: the required areas are visible and creatively integrated, not copied as a list
  • A balance of text and graphics

SPEAK

A ROUND-TABLE DISCUSSION

  • An elevator speech of 2 to 3 minutes
  • A direct answer to one peer question, up to 2 minutes
  • A question submitted for a peer at least 2 hours before their presentation
  • Business casual; professional demeanor

DEEP DIVESpeaker notes: one tool from the sample poster

Dr. Retherford’s sample poster, Technical Presentations: Strategies for Success, recommends one tool: “Speaker notes should be generated independently from the visual complement to the presentation.” Its notes use three colors:

  • Blue: planning notes. Title, purpose, major point.
  • Black: content to say. A short reminder for each point.
  • Green: action to do. For example, “Remember to gesture to slide.”

You can ask your agent to format its speaker notes the same way.

DEEP DIVEPrepare for the round-table
  • Rehearse your elevator speech out loud with a timer: 2 minutes minimum, 3 maximum.
  • Practise answering a question in one direct reply. The sheet puts it this way: “The purpose of this exercise is to answer the question directly, not as a function of time.”
  • Write your peer question early, and submit it through Google Drive at least 2 hours before your peer presents.
  • Be ready for one peer review: the form is handed out on the day, with no advance notice.
  • Read the agent’s content as if you had never seen your papers. If an area of the poster needs you there to explain it, it does not stand alone yet.
DEEP DIVEAI use and responsibility

For this module’s activities you may use AI to draft and check content, and upload course documents and your own papers to UT AI Hub, within university and course data rules. Disclose your AI use as the course requires.

  • I verified every technical claim.
  • I can explain every part of the poster without the agent.
  • I disclosed my AI use as the course requires.

Narration

You could open an AI chat and type one request. Or you could build an agent for this task. Here is the difference.

First, the one-time prompt: make a poster about my WA02 paper. The AI starts writing at once. It never asked what the assignment requires, whether this is for slides or a poster, or how long you will speak, so it guessed.

Check it against the three circles. Instructions: it never saw the sheet or the rubric. Format: a page of paragraphs, not a poster. Speak: no notes, and no time limit. And next time, you would have to explain everything again.

Now the agent. Its instructions are saved once and used every time: it asks for your files, asks slides or a poster, runs the three-circle check, and never invents. You give it the same request, with WA01, WA02, and the Presentation 02 sheet and rubric.

Before it drafts anything, it asks. The Presentation 02 sheet calls for a digital poster. Content for a poster? Yes.

Then it drafts the poster areas and the speaker notes from the same content, and checks all three circles. Every required element is mapped, the areas stand alone, and the notes fit a talk of 2 to 3 minutes.

The prompt guessed once. The agent asks, checks, and you can use it again for your next presentation. You still verify every claim and design the poster yourself.

Build the agent once. Use it every time.
